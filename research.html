Research Statement

Standard RL agents are powerful optimizers, but they face key limitations when AI must work alongside people. First, they offer limited mechanisms for human communication: standard RL agents do not natively support natural-language instructions at inference time—expressing new goals or correcting behavior typically requires modifying the reward function and retraining from scratch. Second, even when objectives are carefully specified, the behavior distributions RL agents learn can be misaligned with human intent: optimizing a proxy reward may produce policies whose outputs diverge from what a human would recognize as correct or desirable. My research addresses both challenges through language-conditioned policy learning and intent-aligned generation, using procedural content generation (PCG) as a structured testbed where misalignment between specification and output is directly measurable.

"Can a single policy, conditioned on natural language, satisfy diverse objectives at inference time—without reward re-engineering—and transfer its learned representation to structurally distinct environments it has never been trained on?"

Research Thrusts
Three thrusts of generalization in language-conditioned RL: (i) reward-free policy conditioning via natural-language goal specifications; (ii) multi-modal conditioning to reduce semantic underspecification; and (iii) a cross-environment shared encoder for language-guided latent interpolation.
Thrust 1 · Language-Conditioned Policy

Natural Language as a Goal Specification Mechanism for Reward-Free Policy Conditioning

Can natural language replace a fixed reward function—conditioning policy behavior at inference time, without retraining?

Standard RL policies are coupled to a fixed training objective and do not readily generalize to novel goals without retraining. We address this by conditioning the policy directly on language: a natural-language instruction is encoded by a pre-trained language model and injected as a conditioning signal into the policy network, producing behaviorally distinct outputs by varying the instruction alone—without retraining at inference time [1].

We extend this to the multi-objective setting: rather than collapsing competing objectives into a scalar reward, we learn a disentangled instruction representation that encourages objectives to occupy separable subspaces. This allows the policy to explore trade-offs across conflicting constraints by interpolating within the instruction embedding space, without retraining for each configuration [2].

Thrust 2 · Multi-Modal Policy Conditioning

Reducing Semantic Underspecification via Tri-Modal Shared Representation

Language alone is ambiguous. Adding a complementary visual modality sharpens policy conditioning and reduces output variance.

A text prompt is semantically underspecified: the same instruction is consistent with many distinct output distributions, limiting the precision of language-only conditioning. We address this by constructing a tri-modal shared representation that jointly embeds three signal types—natural-language instructions, spatial layout observations, and output trajectories—into a unified metric space via contrastive objectives.

The policy accepts any subset of available modalities as conditioning input; the joint embedding space encourages representations from different modalities corresponding to the same behavioral target to cluster together, while semantically distinct targets are pushed apart. Combining text and spatial modalities reduces output variance relative to either modality alone in our evaluations, with human preference studies confirming improved alignment between the specified objective and the resulting behavior [3].

Thrust 3 · Cross-Domain Transfer

Cross-Environment Shared Encoder for Zero-Shot Latent Interpolation

Can one encoder generalize across structurally distinct environments—and support language-guided interpolation between them?

Thrusts 1–2 operate within a single environment with fixed dynamics. We generalize to the multi-task setting by training a shared encoder simultaneously across multiple structurally distinct environments. A cross-environment alignment objective brings latent representations of semantically equivalent behavioral targets together across domains, while preserving environment-specific structure elsewhere in the embedding space.

A language instruction then parameterizes a soft interpolation over the shared latent space, producing outputs that compositionally blend characteristics from distinct source environments— without environment-specific fine-tuning [4]. We see this as a step toward compositional policy generalization: extending the shared encoder to control policies could allow language-guided latent interpolation to produce behavioral blends, potentially supporting transfer to task combinations not seen during training.
